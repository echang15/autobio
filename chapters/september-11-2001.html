September 2001 · Sophomore year

September 11, 2001

Written in September 2026, ahead of the 25th anniversary, while the memory is still sharp.

I was a sophomore at the University of Delaware, sitting in Data Structures. I always sat in the back — a lazy slacker's seat, the kind you pick specifically so the professor doesn't call on you. It was an entirely ordinary Tuesday morning right up until it wasn't.

Near the end of class, one of my classmates spoke up and announced that a plane had hit the first tower. I remember exactly what I thought: total BS. I figured he was making some kind of sick joke, and I didn't think about it again until I got back to my dorm.

I turned on the TV and saw the smoking tower, and that's when it became real. I was watching live when the second plane hit — I saw the second tower get hit in real time. I went into a complete state of shock.

It wasn't until a little later that I remembered my cousin was in one of the towers. That's when the shock turned into immediate panic. He's my first cousin on my mother's side — his mom is my mom's sister, and he's a bit older than me. He normally lives in Taiwan, like most of my extended family; I'm the one who ended up in the US. He happened to be in Manhattan on business that day, due to be in one of the lower buildings in the World Trade Center complex. He got out before the towers came down, but leaving the island afterward was its own ordeal — the bridges and all the traffic out of Manhattan were completely deadlocked. My dad drove all the way up to North Jersey to get him. I don't know the exact details of how they connected — whether it was across a bridge or part of the boat evacuations that day — that part is really my dad's story to tell, not mine. But I remember the waiting, and not sleeping, until we knew he was safe.

I didn't sleep that night. At some point I ended up at a diner on Main Street with my freshman-year roommate and a friend, mostly just to have something to do besides sit with it. A couple of people at a nearby table were talking about the news — trying to make sense of how someone could jump out of a window. My friends noticed how distraught I was getting overhearing that, and one of them walked over and quietly told them about my cousin. They stopped talking about it after that.

The Days After

The university held a candlelight vigil in the days that followed. Pretty much the entire campus turned out — everyone was in shock together. Some good friends of mine, people I'd met through Summer College back in 1999, showed up with a lot of empathy and just spent time with me. I really appreciated that. It changed the tone of that whole school year.

Years Later, on Long Island

This all came flying back to me years later. After I graduated in 2004, I ended up working in New York for a small international logistics company in Jamaica, Queens, right by JFK Airport. As a hobby, I picked up beer-league ice hockey — a midnight league that traveled between rinks all over Long Island, playing against other people who just did it for the love of the sport.

Sometime between 2004 and 2006, I was at a rink in Freeport with my team, the Blues, and I noticed a couple of jerseys hanging up from a firefighting team. I asked one of my new teammates what the story was. He got quiet for a second and told me that team used to be made up entirely of firefighters — and that they'd lost most of the entire team on September 11th.
